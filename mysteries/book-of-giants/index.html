The Dead Sea Scrolls · Qumran

The Book of Giants

A Living Investigation

The manuscripts are real.The story is fragmentary.

Ancient fragments of a lost story about giants survived among the Dead Sea Scrolls. Some words can still be read. Others have been reconstructed by scholars. This investigation separates what survives from what has been restored, interpreted—and reconstructed for our films.

Book of Giants · Qumran Cave 4

Physical manuscript

What it proves

Ancient manuscript fragments belonging to a work scholars identify as the Book of Giants physically survive among the Dead Sea Scrolls.

What it doesn't prove

The surviving manuscripts do not establish that giants historically existed or that the supernatural events narrated in the ancient story occurred.

A surviving fragment is not a complete story.

The Book of Giants · Chapter I

Ohyah

A giant dreamed that judgment was coming.

From the surviving Aramaic Dead Sea Scrolls

Two giant brothers, Hahyah and Ohyah, receive disturbing dreams. Ohyah's vision carries him into a heavenly judgment scene: the Ruler of Heaven descends, thrones are established, books are opened and judgment is pronounced. The giants are terrified—and send Mahaway to seek an interpretation from Enoch.

Scanned infrared negative

Aramaic and fragmentary

Specialist manuscript profiles identify 4Q530 as one of the better-preserved Book of Giants witnesses, while stressing that its surviving text remains incomplete.

Enoch's answer receipt · 4Q203 Fragment 8

Part of his answer survived.

What survives

4Q203 Fragment 8 preserves material associated with a second tablet or letter connected with Enoch. It addresses the Watchers and refers to deeds and corruption involving them and their offspring.

[Restored text]

The fragment is damaged. Portions of its destruction and judgment language have been editorially reconstructed by scholars; brackets in critical readings must not be silently removed.

Scholarly interpretation

Scholars understand Enoch's message as announcing condemnation, punishment, or destruction involving the Watchers and giants. The complete interpretation of Ohyah's dream does not survive intact.

Artist reconstruction

The companion Reel turns this fragmentary textual material into a continuous dramatic encounter. Its dialogue and physical staging are not presented as a verbatim translation.

Record mapping: the IAA labels B-295415 “Plate 906, Frag 4” and identifies it as 4Q203. Marro's critical edition maps that imaged object to manuscript fragment 8.

Where the investigation now stands

What the evidence supports—and what it does not.

Supported by the ancient textual tradition

  • The Book of Giants existed in antiquity.
  • Ohyah is named in the surviving material.
  • Ohyah and Hahyah experience disturbing dreams.
  • The giants react with fear.
  • Mahaway is sent to Enoch seeking interpretation.
  • Enoch is associated with a written response or tablet.
  • Surviving material concerns the deeds and corruption of the Watchers and giant offspring.
  • Judgment, punishment, or destruction forms part of the surviving and reconstructed textual context.

Some wording in the damaged passages depends on scholarly restoration and must remain labeled as such.

Not established

  • The exact appearance or height of Ohyah or Mahaway.
  • The exact appearance of Enoch.
  • The precise physical setting of their encounters.
  • That the assembled giants physically stood or knelt before Enoch.
  • Exact continuous dialogue used in the cinematic reconstruction.
  • The historical occurrence of the supernatural events described by the ancient story.

The existence of an ancient manuscript establishes the existence of the ancient story—not the historical occurrence of its supernatural events.
